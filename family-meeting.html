The meeting we actually run

Once a week, we sit down at the kitchen table for twenty minutes and go through the same five parts, in the same order. This is a real one, filled in — not a blank template with the hard parts left out.

1. Check-in

3 minutes

One sentence each: how are you walking in tonight? Dad goes first, because if I won’t say it out loud, nobody else will either.

“Long week. Glad it’s Friday.”

“Okay, just hungry.”

2. Highs and lows

5 minutes

One high and one low from the week, each person. No fixing, no lectures — just hear it. This is where the stuff comes out that never shows up at dinner.

High: “Our team won on Saturday.”

Low: “I left my project until Sunday night and it was awful.”

3. The week ahead

5 minutes

Games, practices, deadlines, who needs a ride where. If it’s said here, nobody gets to say “nobody told me” on Thursday.

“Practice Tuesday and Thursday, so dinner is earlier those nights.”

“The car goes to the shop Wednesday — plan rides around it.”

4. One thing to try

4 minutes

We pick one small thing to try for the next seven days. One — not five. It only has to survive a week, and next week we say honestly whether it did.

This week’s: “Phones charge in the kitchen overnight. Everybody. Parents too.”

5. Thank-you round

3 minutes

Each person thanks one person for one specific thing. Small and specific beats big and general, every time. Then we’re done: “Thank you for being here. Same time next week.”

“Thanks for clearing the table on Thursday without being asked.”

Honest part: this is an experiment I’m still running in my own house, not a system that fixed it. Some weeks it runs long. Some weeks nobody wants to talk, and two of us do most of it. We keep sitting down anyway — a short, messy meeting every week beats a perfect one that happens once.

The meeting is where our family agreement started. Inside The Family Ground, members write those agreements down together — the rules, and what happens when they’re kept and when they’re not — and everyone signs.
